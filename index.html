Physics-Inspired AI
for Scientific Discovery

From molecules to materials, we ask how AI can scale without losing the physical structure that makes scientific prediction reliable.

Φ
01 / The premise

What should AI learn, and what should physics guarantee?

Scientific AI is entering the foundation-model era. Yet molecules and materials must obey symmetries, conservation laws, energetic stability, and constraints that scale alone may not recover.

PhiSci brings together machine learning, chemistry, physics, and materials science to examine where physical structure belongs in the next generation of scientific models.

MoleculesMaterials
02 / Core questions

Three tensions.
One shared frontier.

01

Structure × Scale

Physical inductive bias under data scarcity

Which priors should be hard-coded, softly encouraged, or learned from data? How does that balance change as datasets and models grow?

  • Equivariance + symmetry
  • Conservation laws
  • Governing equations
02

Plausible × Physical

Validity beyond distributional fidelity

A generated structure can look right and still be unstable. What should count as physically meaningful across molecules and materials?

  • Energetic stability
  • Dynamics + uncertainty
  • Simulation + experiment
03

Domains × Scales

Unified physical representations

Can common geometric and physical knowledge transfer across biomolecules, crystals, disordered matter, and continuum descriptions?

  • Cross-domain transfer
  • Multiscale learning
  • Foundation models

The question behind PhiSci

How should physics-inspired learning evolve, rather than disappear, as scientific AI enters the foundation-model regime?
