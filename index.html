As AI for molecules and materials scales, where should physical knowledge enter learning: in the architecture, as the training signal, or through experiment?

Φ
01 / The premise

Where should physical knowledge enter learning?

Foundation models are moving the bottleneck beyond static labels. What remains scarce are correct ensembles, rare events, accuracy beyond the underlying theory, and agreement with sparse or indirect measurements.

PhiSci asks how physical knowledge should shape representations, supply learning objectives, and connect scientific models to experimental ground truth.

02 / Core questions

Three entry points.
One learning loop.

01

Physics as architecture

Which structure must be built in?

When should symmetry, conservation, locality, and electronic structure be hard-coded, softly encouraged, or learned through augmentation and scale?

  • Equivariance + symmetry
  • Energies + forces
  • Electronic Hamiltonians
02

Physics as training signal

Learning from energies and equations

Can generative models learn from energy functions, forces, and self-consistency conditions rather than labeled simulation data?

  • Boltzmann sampling
  • Diffusion + control
  • Label-free objectives
03

Experiment as ground truth

Aligning models with measurement

How should models learn from sparse, noisy, indirect, or ensemble-averaged observables, especially when simulation and experiment disagree?

  • Data fusion
  • Inverse problems
  • Physical validity

The question behind PhiSci

What should we build in, what should we optimize against, and what should scientific models align with?

05 / Call for papers

Planned non-archival track

Work in progress is welcome.

Submissions will be double-blind through OpenReview using the ICLR 2027 template. All accepted papers will be presented as posters, with a balanced subset selected for contributed talks.

01

Full papers

Up to 4 pages, excluding references and appendices, for ongoing or recently completed work.

02

Tiny papers

Up to 2 pages for focused ideas, modest results, re-analysis, benchmarks, datasets, negative results, or early experiment-alignment work.

Submissions are non-archival. Tiny papers must be primarily human-authored. Previously published or accepted machine-learning conference papers, including the ICLR 2027 main track, are not eligible.

  4. Feb 22, 2027Decisions sent to authors
