Proposed ICLR 2027 Workshop

Physics-Inspired AI for Scientific Discovery: From Molecules to Materials

San Francisco, CA · April 29 or 30, 2027 (tentative)

About

Machine learning for molecules and materials has entered a foundation-model regime: generative models design crystals across the periodic table, universal interatomic potentials span molecules, materials, and interfaces, and large-scale transfer now reaches electronic Hamiltonians. What remains scarce is what a static label cannot provide: accuracy beyond the underlying theory, ensembles and rare events that must be sampled, and agreement with sparse, indirect experiments. A model can be accurate configuration by configuration yet produce the wrong ensemble, and an ensemble can be faithful to DFT yet disagree with measurement. PhiSci brings together machine learning, chemistry, physics, and materials science to ask where physical knowledge should enter learning:

1Physics as architecture

Which physical structure should be built in, and which can be learned from data and scale?

  • Equivariance & symmetry breaking
  • Conservative vs. direct forces
  • Built-in constraints vs. augmentation
  • Locality
  • Electronic Hamiltonians
  • Inductive biases at scale

2Physics as the training signal

When can energies and physical equations replace labeled data?

  • Boltzmann generators
  • Diffusion samplers
  • Stochastic optimal control
  • Energy-based rewards & guidance
  • Rare events & enhanced sampling
  • Free energies & kinetics
  • Generative coarse-graining
  • Neural wavefunctions & self-consistent DFT

3Experiment as ground truth

How should models be aligned with, and evaluated against, real measurements?

  • Fine-tuning to experimental observables
  • Simulation–experiment data fusion
  • Generative priors for inverse problems
  • Diffraction, scattering & spectroscopy
  • Learning from corrupted observations
  • Spectra, bands & transport
  • Ensemble-level validity benchmarks

Panel: How much scientific prior do we need in AI for science?

  • Does the bitter lesson apply to science, or is scientific data fundamentally too scarce?
  • Do physics priors accelerate discovery, or impose a ceiling on what AI can discover?
  • When theory and experiment disagree, should AI trust the data over first principles?
  • Will general-purpose foundation models replace expert scientific models, or does domain knowledge remain indispensable?

Call for Papers

  • Full papers: up to 4 pages, excluding references and appendices.
  • Tiny papers: up to 2 pages, excluding references. Negative results, sampler benchmarks, datasets of experimental observables, and early experiment-alignment results are welcome.
  • ICLR 2027 template, double-blind review on OpenReview (link TBA).
  • Non-archival. Papers already accepted at an ML conference, including the ICLR 2027 main track, are not eligible.
